# PLOS ONE — Generative-AI Disclosure Policy

**Source:** PLOS Ethical Publishing Practice — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/ethical-publishing-practice
**Fetched:** 2026-05-06

## Core rules

1. **AI tools cannot be listed as authors or contributors.** Authorship requires accountability that an AI cannot provide.
2. **Authors are accountable for everything in the manuscript.** Statements of hypotheses, interpretations, results, conclusions, limitations, and implications must represent the authors' own ideas, regardless of whether an LLM produced or assisted in drafting the prose.
3. **Confidentiality during peer review.** Editors and reviewers must NOT upload submission content to generative-AI platforms, websites, databases, or other tools.

## Required disclosure

When an AI tool was used in any way that materially affected the study, the manuscript content, the data, or supporting files, the authors must disclose:

- **Name and version** of each AI tool used.
- **Description of how** the tool was used.
- **How the authors evaluated the validity** of the tool's outputs.
- **Which aspects** of the study, manuscript, data, or supporting files were affected or generated by AI.

## Where to put the disclosure

Place it in a **dedicated sub-section of Methods** titled e.g. "Use of generative AI tools."
For article types that lack a Methods section, place the disclosure in the **Acknowledgments**.

## Suggested disclosure template (Methods sub-section)

> **Use of generative AI tools.** We used [tool name and version, e.g. ChatGPT-4 (OpenAI)] to [exact use, e.g. "draft an initial outline of the Introduction" / "generate candidate variable names for code refactoring" / "translate text from Portuguese to English"]. We evaluated the validity of the tool's outputs by [evaluation procedure, e.g. "manually verifying every cited reference against the original source" / "running the regenerated code against our existing test suite" / "having a bilingual co-author check the translation against the source"]. The following aspects of the manuscript or supporting materials were affected by this use: [list, e.g. "Sections of the Introduction were drafted with AI assistance and then revised by the authors. No data, results, figures, or analyses were generated by AI."]. All scientific claims, interpretations, and conclusions are the authors' own.

## Suggested disclosure template (Acknowledgments — for article types without Methods)

> The authors acknowledge use of [tool name and version] for [purpose]. Outputs were validated by [procedure]. [Affected components.] All authors take full responsibility for the content of this article.

## Negative declaration (recommended when no AI was used)

> No generative AI tools were used in any aspect of the design, execution, analysis, or writing of this study.

## Notes for the writing pipeline

- Track every AI-assisted writing event (which paragraph, which tool, which version).
- When using AI to draft prose, run the `humanizer` skill before submission and re-verify every citation independently — do **not** trust LLM-supplied references.
- Do not run the manuscript through generative-AI peer-review or summarization services after submission; this violates PLOS confidentiality rules even if you are an author.
